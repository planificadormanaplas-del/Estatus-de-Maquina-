# AI tells to avoid in LinkedIn posts

Adapted from Wikipedia's *Signs of AI writing* guide. These are the patterns that most reliably mark text as AI-generated. When drafting, scan for these and strip them out. Most of the time, the fix is to delete the sentence rather than rewrite it.

---

## Vocabulary tells

LLMs over-use a specific set of words far more than humans do. None of these are wrong on their own, but together they form an unmistakable pattern. Even one or two of these can read as AI in a short post.

**Verbs:** delve, leverage, navigate, ensure, foster, cultivate, streamline, underscore, showcase, highlight (as a verb), align with, garner, encompass, bolster, unlock

**Adjectives:** robust, seamless, holistic, comprehensive, vibrant, intricate, pivotal, crucial, valuable, key, enduring, meticulous, transformative

**Phrases to cut on sight:**
- "valuable insights"
- "key takeaways"
- "play a vital role"
- "in today's landscape" / "in today's fast-paced world"
- "in the realm of"
- "stands as" / "serves as"
- "is a testament to"
- "navigate the complexities of"
- "at the forefront of"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "synergy"

## Structural tells

**Negative parallelism.** "It's not just X — it's Y." "This isn't about X, it's about Y." This is one of the most reliable AI signatures. Almost never appears in real personal-voice writing. Cut it.

**Rule of three.** Lists of three parallel adjectives or noun phrases ("clear, concise, and actionable" / "innovative, scalable, and impactful"). LLMs reach for triplets constantly. Real writers tend to write specific singular things instead.

**Em dash for punched-up emphasis.** Em dashes are fine as ordinary punctuation, but the *formulaic* use — clause break, dramatic restatement, summing up — is a strong AI tell. Compare:
- AI-shaped: "We need to act now — not tomorrow, not next week, but today."
- Human-shaped: just say "We need to act now."

**Bulleted lists with bold inline headers.** "**Background:** ... **Next steps:** ... **Timeline:** ..." This is a slide-deck format pretending to be a post. If three points genuinely need separation, use three short paragraphs instead.

**Title Case Headings inside short posts.** Almost always wrong in post body text. If a heading is needed at all (rare for a LinkedIn post), sentence case.

**Excessive boldface for emphasis.** Especially on the same word every time it appears, or on opening words of bullets. Plain text is almost always better.

**"In summary" / "In conclusion" / "Overall" closings.** A short post doesn't need a wrap-up sentence. The last substantive sentence IS the closing. Just stop writing.

**Engagement-bait closings.** "What do you think? Drop a comment!" "Agree or disagree? Let me know!" "Tag someone who needs to hear this." These reek of formula. If a post earns a conversation, it earns it on substance.

**Single-sentence line stacks.**
"Like.
This.
Where.
Every line.
Is its own paragraph."

This is a LinkedIn-influencer convention that LLMs love to mimic. Use it sparingly if at all — most personal-voice posts read better as actual paragraphs.

## Tone tells

**Constructed humor.** Setup-punchline jokes. "Wordplay" that's clearly engineered. Real humor in personal-voice writing usually comes from noticing something specific and saying it flatly — not from constructing a joke. If a line *feels* like a setup for a punchline, it probably is one, and it'll read as AI.

**Excess enthusiasm.** "Awesome!" "Fantastic!" "Wonderful news!" "Thrilled to share!" "Excited to announce!" Real warmth is quieter than this.

**Performative gratitude.** "Thank you SO much for..." "I really, really appreciate..." A single "Thanks" usually does the job.

**Reassurance no one asked for.** "Please rest assured that..." "I want to assure you..." If the audience didn't raise a worry, don't manufacture one to soothe.

**Apologizing reflexively.** "Apologies for the long post" / "Sorry if this is rambling" when neither is real. Skip it unless there's an actual thing to apologize for.

## Formatting tells

**Curly quotation marks (" " ' ').** ChatGPT outputs these by default; Claude generally doesn't, but worth checking. Use straight quotes (" " ' ') unless the user specifically wants otherwise.

**Excessive markdown.** Bold sprinkled everywhere, headers in posts that don't need them, bullet points for things that should be sentences. Plain prose by default.

**Stock emojis at the start of bullets** (✅, 🚀, 💡 on every line). Either use emojis sparingly and naturally, or not at all.

---

## The single best test

Read the draft out loud. If any sentence sounds like it could've been written by a moderately competent corporate communications consultant for any executive at any company — that sentence is wrong. Personal voice lives in specificity. The post should sound like *the user* talking, not like a polished generic professional.
